Growth plan for all three channels

Built from the study of the 19 Shorts in the "EVER THOUGHT WHY" playlist and our own numbers (19 Nov 2026). No paid services. Two places do the work: GitHub (free, every day) and this laptop (free, graphics card, kept on).

The five rules every video follows, on every channel

  1. A story, not a fact list. A hidden thing revealed, "you are" someone, or "you" in a real situation. This is what the three explainer hits share; the eight beautiful Shorts without a story all stayed under 3,000 views.
  2. The first 2 seconds make people need the answer. A surprising true statement, or a question about something hidden. Never the title read out.
  3. Every picture shows what is being said, and changes every 3 to 4 seconds. No fillers.
  4. The voice flows. Hits pause 1 to 2 times a minute; ours about 27. Sentences run into each other.
  5. One question at the end, then a reason to come back: the last line leads back into the first (the video loops), or "part 2 tomorrow" when there is one. No countdown, no "drop your guess".

Two production lines

Line A: 3D stories (this laptop)

  • Blender, fully animated by script, on the RTX 4050 (0.65 s a frame, about 13 minutes for a 40-second Short).
  • One channel mascot (simple, round-headed, like the Jensen Short) and reusable sets: inside the body, kitchen, ocean, space, lab, road, engine room.
  • Free models only: Poly Haven, Kenney, Quaternius, Smithsonian 3D scans, NASA, real molecules from the Protein Data Bank.
  • For topics a set can show. Starts with 2 to 3 a week, more as the sets grow.

Line B: real footage (GitHub)

  • What we have now, fixed: each line gets a clip that shows exactly that thing (Pexels, Pixabay, NASA, ESO, Smithsonian, Wikimedia), a new clip every 3 to 4 seconds, one big word on screen.
  • If no clip shows the thing, the line is rewritten to something we can show.
  • For every topic the 3D sets do not cover. Keeps the daily schedule full.

Each channel's place

Ever Thought Why? (English, US viewers)

Hidden science in your body, kitchen and animals. Our best numbers already come from here (pruney fingers, pineapple, tuna vs cod).

  • 3D: inside-the-body and kitchen stories with the mascot.
  • Footage: animal and food reveals.
  • Voice: E2 male, flowing.

Why It Works (English, STEM)

How machines and everyday tech work inside. 3D cut-aways fit this channel best: engines, locks, microwaves, phones, bridges.

  • 3D: the engine-room and machine sets, x-ray views (like the "no engine oil" Short, plus a story).
  • Voice: A1 female, flowing.

Aakhir Kyun? (Hindi, Indian viewers)

Real Indian situations, told as a story. Fog on highways, trains, monsoon, cricket, festivals, food: the 3D Wala Guru way (527 thousand views on a 4,400-subscriber channel).

  • Hinglish with "तुम", opening with a real event, practical ("what to do").
  • 3D: the road and city sets.
  • Voice: H14 (Swara) stays; worth one test of a lively male Hindi voice against it.

More reach, beyond the video itself

  • Series: topics with a part 2 end with "part 2 tomorrow", and each series gets its own playlist (already automatic).
  • One look per channel: the same mascot, colours and caption style every time, so a viewer knows it is us in the first second.
  • Posting at each audience's hour: US evening for the English channels, Indian evening (7 to 9 PM) for Hindi.
  • Learn every week: what kept viewers in our own videos feeds the next scripts (already running), plus a weekly check of new hits in the playlist you keep adding to.
  • Optional, needs your accounts: the same Shorts on Instagram Reels and Facebook. Only if you want; it needs you to sign in there.
