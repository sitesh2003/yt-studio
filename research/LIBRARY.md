## Why Ponds Don't Freeze Solid 🤔
*2026-11-15 · How Things Work*

- Water reaches its maximum density at approximately 4 °C (39 °F).
- Liquid water cooler than 4 °C becomes less dense and rises toward the surface, freezing into an insulating layer of ice.
- The ice layer acts as a thermal insulator, preventing the underlying water from freezing solid and allowing aquatic life to survive.
- Watermelons float because their overall average density is lower than that of liquid water.

## Why Heat Treating Makes Steel Hard 🤯
*2026-11-15 · How Things Work*

- At room temperature, iron atoms form a ferrite crystal structure where planes can slip past each other.
- Heating iron transforms it into austenite, dissolving carbon atoms in the face-centered cubic structure.
- Rapid quenching freezes the carbon in place, forming martensite, which is a stressed body-centered tetragonal structure that resists dislocation movement and increases hardness.

## Why Highway Guardrails Have Heavy End Boxes 🤯
*2026-11-14 · How Things Work*

- Guardrail end terminals (extruder terminals) flatten and curl steel W-beams upon impact.
- Plastic deformation of the guardrail absorbs the kinetic energy of a crashing vehicle.
- Extruder terminals are engineered to redirect the flattened beam away from the roadway to prevent vehicle spearing.

## Why Your Funny Bone Shocks You ⚡

## Why Car Windshields Have Black Dots 🤔
*2026-11-14 · Everyday Whys*

- The black border and dots on a windshield are a ceramic paint called frit.
- Frit helps bake the adhesive that bonds the windshield to the car frame.
- The dot pattern (halftone) helps distribute thermal stress to prevent the glass from cracking due to temperature differentials between the dark ceramic and clear glass.

## How Tuning Forks Actually Make Sound 🔊
*2026-11-14 · How Things Work*

- Tuning forks have two prongs that vibrate in opposite directions, creating a balanced mechanical system that minimizes stem vibration.
- A single metal rod vibrates differently and lacks the counter-balancing motion of paired prongs.
- Scissors use a shearing mechanism created by two crossing blades rather than a single downward wedge.

## The Hidden Cause Of Brain Freeze 😮
*2026-11-14 · Body Whys*

- Brain freeze (sphenopalatine ganglioneuralgia) is caused by rapid cooling and warming of the palate capillaries.
- The pain is referred pain, misinterpreted by the trigeminal nerve.
- The trigeminal nerve carries sensory signals from the face and mouth to the brain.
- Pressing the tongue against the palate is a widely accepted technique to alleviate the sensation by providing warmth.
