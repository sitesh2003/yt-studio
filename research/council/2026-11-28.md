# Council, 28 Nov 2026: the first five seconds, and a better 3D look

Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini (both signed in as the owner) ran two rounds. The owner asked for it: "in 1 second which hook... 2 seconds which hook... the first scene is most important, otherwise people will just scroll", and "increase our 3D rendering styles... more accurate or more detailed".

## The problem everyone agreed on
Our Shorts open on a narrated myth over a slow **establishing shot**: a chilli beside a thermometer, a gecko on glass, a woodpecker pecking. That shows the topic but gives no reason to stay. ChatGPT: "Frame 1 must contain the contradiction, not the introduction."

## Final rules for seconds 0 to 5 (all three agree, ranked)
1. **Cold open on the strongest frame of the whole Short.** Bring the payoff forward. It should also work as the thumbnail.
2. **Start mid-action.** No establishing shot, logo, title card or slow approach.
3. **Break the myth in the first 4 to 6 spoken words.** No "Did you know", and never start with "This...".
4. **Sound on frame 0:** a matching transient (crack, thud, hiss, click, whirr). The first word lands by 0.15 s.
5. **One subject, high contrast, readable on a phone *with the sound off*.**
6. **A dedicated hook shot of 1.5 to 2 s per Short**, rendered on purpose rather than taken from the establishing shot.
7. **Beat sheet:**
   - 0 to 0.5 s: the impossible image.
   - 0.5 to 1 s: one new complication.
   - 1 to 2 s: dive into the mechanism with a big change of scale.
   - 2 to 3.5 s: cause and effect.
   - 3.5 to 5 s: the first proof.
8. **Every cut adds information:** 3 or 4 meaningful changes in 5 s, not random cuts.
9. **On-screen text of 2 to 5 words** in the safe centre, one idea at a time. Normal captions stay.
10. **The last frame loops into frame 1.** Match the motion, the composition or the sound.

Also agreed:
- Use three camera moves only: fast push-in, lateral tracking, and a macro or cutaway dive. No constant orbiting and no aimless drift.
- Vary the visual language from topic to topic so the channel doesn't feel like a template.
- The Hindi first line is written natively, not translated.
- Check the first 5 s on a real phone, at feed size, with sound on and off, before approving a Short.

## Fact check from this council
Both models doubted "ATP synthase turns about 100 times a second". We checked the source:
- **Watt et al. 2010, PNAS 107:16823**, abstract: "the rotation at about 100 times per second of the central stalk".
- **Yasuda et al. 2001, Nature 410:898**: about 130 revolutions per second at saturating ATP.

The claim **holds**. The scheduled ATP films and Shorts stay as they are.

## What to measure (YouTube Studio)
In order:
1. **Stayed to watch** (viewed vs swiped away): tests frame 1 and the first line.
2. **Retention at 1, 3 and 5 s.**
3. **Average % viewed.**
4. **Shares per 1,000 engaged views.**
5. **Subscribers per 1,000.**

How to read the curve:
- A cliff at 1 s means the line doesn't match the picture.
- A drop at 2 to 3 s means the reveal is too slow.
- A drop at 5 to 8 s means it has turned into a lecture.

The 21-day test: three hook styles (contradiction, impossible action, hidden mechanism) over 12 comparable topics, comparing medians at 48 h and 7 days. Each weekly council brings three Shorts: the best by stayed-to-watch, the best by shares per 1,000, and the worst by 3-s retention.

## 3D upgrade order for the next month (EEVEE, Blender 5.1, 6 GB)
1. **Lighting and materials:**
   - Key, rim and bounce on every scene.
   - Procedural roughness and bump micro-detail.
   - Glow only as an intentional signal, never as the default material.
   - Three reusable scene templates: macro biology, animal biomechanics, everyday physics.
2. **Scientific structure and scale:**
   - Gecko: toe pad, setae, spatulae.
   - ATP synthase: F0, rotor, F1.
   - TRPV1: pore and binding site.
   - A scale bar or a familiar object in frame, plus cutaways.
3. **Real proteins from the PDB** (TRPV1, ATP synthase). Molecular Nodes' current release targets Blender 5.2, so test an older release on 5.1 or export the geometry. Animate only the movement the science supports.
4. **Anatomy-first animals:** silhouette and correct parts before polygon count.
5. **Finishing:** restrained depth of field on macro shots, motion blur on fast movers only (rotors, kernels, beaks), 1K to 2K textures, instancing, few transparent layers.

**Avoid:** photorealism for its own sake, volumetrics across the whole scene, heavy subdivision, constant camera motion, and detail nobody can see.
