What the big Shorts do, and what we change

Research on all 19 Shorts in the "EVER THOUGHT WHY" playlist (19 Nov 2026), checked one by one, compared with three of our own Shorts and our three chosen voices, and a plan for polished 3D explainers.

How it was done: every Short was watched as a strip of frames from start to end; its sound was transcribed and measured on this laptop (speaking speed, pauses, how much the voice rises and falls). Views and lengths are from YouTube.

The answer in five lines

  1. Our screen layout is already the same as the 11.9 million Tuna Short (a clip in the middle, blurred copy above and below, words in the middle). Layout is not the problem.
  2. Our pictures do not show what the voice says, and stay too long. In "Fingers prune" an old 1914 photo plays under "rigid shell", a paper fan under "collapses inward", and one clip of a child drinking water stays for about 16 seconds over unrelated words. In the Tuna Short every word has its own matching clip: "tuna" shows tuna, "cans" shows cans.
  3. Our voice stops too often. The hits talk in one continuous stream: about 1 to 2 pauses a minute. Our male voice E2 pauses about 27 times a minute; the public videos about 21 to 22.
  4. 3D animation is real and it works (2.1 million, 527 thousand), but only as a story: a character, a place, things happening. Blender is already installed on this laptop, so a free 3D route exists.
  5. The Hindi hit sounds like a friend telling a story: male, fast, very expressive, uses "तुम", tells a real event. Our Hindi voice is a calmer female voice reading an explanation with "आप".
  6. Polish alone does not bring views. Eight of the 19 are beautifully made (3D molecules, 3D computer parts, neon code graphics, flat 2D cartoons, a chalkboard) and every one of them is under 3,000 views. What the three explainer hits share is a story: a hidden thing revealed (Tuna), "you are" a person (Jensen), "you are" in danger (fog). Beauty is the floor, the story is what spreads.

What this means

  • English: E2's speed is fine. What separates it from the 11.9 M Short is the stopping: it breaks after almost every sentence. The hits run sentences into each other. Fix: shorter gaps between sentences (the voice step already has a setting for this) and scripts written as one flowing story, not separate facts.
  • Expressive voices win stories: the Jensen and 3D Wala Guru narrators rise and fall about twice as much as ours. For a story format, E2's liveliness setting can go up a step; check it stays clear.
  • Hindi: Swara flows well (no stops) but is flatter and calmer than the Hindi hit, and it is a female voice where the hit is a male storyteller. Script changes that cost nothing: use "तुम", open with a real event ("बुल्गारिया के हाईवे पर 86 गाड़ियाँ…"), and tell it as a story. Hindi words read badly in our sample (for example "fingers" came out close to "फिंगल्स") also need a pronunciation check.

How the hits open

  • Tuna: "Ever wonder exactly what's hiding inside the fish we eat all the time?" A question, but about something hidden the viewer wants to see, not the title repeated.
  • Jensen: "You are Jensen Huang, the CEO of NVIDIA…" Puts the viewer inside the story.
  • 3D Wala Guru: "In fog you follow the red light of the car ahead…" Puts the viewer in a real danger they know.
  • Ours (best): "Fresh pineapple is literally eating you back." Strong. Ours (weak): "Why do potatoes sprout shoots from those tiny dents?" The title read out again.

Matches the rule set today: a strong opening that makes people need the answer; a question only when it points at something hidden, never the title repeated.

What to change, in order

  1. Voice flow (this week): cut the gap between sentences for E2 and A1 to close to the hits' level; check every video still passes the listening check.
  2. Pictures that match the words (this week): a new clip at least every 3 to 4 seconds; each clip must show the thing being said, or the line is rewritten; no old photos or unrelated objects as fillers.
  3. Hindi script style (this week): "तुम", a real event to open, told as a story.
  4. A series hook at the end, like "next part tomorrow", for topics that have a part 2, alongside the one closing question.
  5. 3D story test (later, needs your yes): a Blender pipeline with free models and simple round-headed characters, like the 2.1 M Jensen Short. I would show it only if it truly looks as good as that one.

Polished 3D explainers: how we would do it

Tested today on this laptop: Blender 5.1 renders a full phone-size frame (1080 × 1920) in 0.65 seconds on the RTX 4050 graphics card. A 40-second Short is about 1,200 frames, so about 13 minutes to render. The free GitHub machines that make our videos now have no graphics card, so 3D videos must render on this laptop.

We can do (free, open source)

  • Blender, run by script: scenes, cameras, lights, moving parts, x-ray and cut-away views, slow zooms from outside to inside, particle swarms (blood cells, molecules, raindrops), simple physics.
  • One channel mascot: a simple round-headed character like the Jensen Short's, built once and reused, with poses and faces (happy, shocked, thinking). Mouth shapes follow the voice with Rhubarb Lip Sync (free, open source).
  • Free 3D models with a licence we can use on a paid channel:
    • Poly Haven (CC0): models, lighting, surfaces
    • Kenney and Quaternius (CC0): low-poly props and animated characters with walk, run and wave moves
    • ambientCG (CC0): surfaces
    • Smithsonian 3D (CC0): scans of real fossils, skulls, animals
    • NASA 3D Resources: spacecraft and planets
    • RCSB Protein Data Bank with Molecular Nodes (free Blender add-on): real molecules, animated
  • Scene kits built once and reused: inside the body, a kitchen, the ocean, space, a lab, a road with cars, a factory line. Each kit has ready camera moves (fly-in, x-ray, side-by-side, size comparison).
  • A frame check: every finished render is turned into a strip of frames like the ones in this study and checked against the Jensen and 3D Wala Guru frames before it can be posted.

We cannot do, or not well

  • A brand-new 3D world for any topic, made fully automatically, at this quality. When an AI writes a whole Blender scene from nothing, it looks cheap. The channels that look good reuse their own sets. So 3D covers the topics our kits cover; other topics stay on real footage.
  • Realistic humans (and our rule says never photo-real AI anyway).
  • Rendering on GitHub. 3D needs this laptop switched on at a fixed time each day, or a paid cloud graphics card.
  • Fluids and smoke at film quality: possible, but each takes hours to calculate. We would fake them with simpler effects.

The plan, with a quality gate

  1. One hero Short by hand, frame by frame: one topic, the "inside the body" kit, the mascot, the E2 voice. I compare its frames side by side with the Jensen and 3D Wala Guru frames. You see it only if it truly matches; if not, I tell you what is missing.
  2. Turn it into kits: the same scenes and camera moves, reusable for any body topic; then the kitchen, ocean and space kits.
  3. Connect to the daily run: the script writer marks topics a kit can show; the laptop renders them; everything else stays on matched real footage.

Already done today

  • No "Pause and guess, three, two, one" on any channel; no question in the middle; one closing question with nothing after it.
  • The first line must be a strong, surprising true statement or a question about something hidden; the title read out again is sent back.
  • The guardrail Short was taken off today's schedule; 7 banked countdown videos are held back and queued to be made again without the countdown.
