What the big Shorts do, and what we change

Research on the 18 Shorts in the "EVER THOUGHT WHY" playlist (19 Nov 2026), compared with three of our own Shorts and our three chosen voices.

How it was done: every Short was watched as a strip of frames from start to end; its sound was transcribed and measured on this laptop (speaking speed, pauses, how much the voice rises and falls). Views and lengths are from YouTube.

The answer in five lines

  1. Our screen layout is already the same as the 11.9 million Tuna Short (a clip in the middle, blurred copy above and below, words in the middle). Layout is not the problem.
  2. Our pictures do not show what the voice says, and stay too long. In "Fingers prune" an old 1914 photo plays under "rigid shell", a paper fan under "collapses inward", and one clip of a child drinking water stays for about 16 seconds over unrelated words. In the Tuna Short every word has its own matching clip: "tuna" shows tuna, "cans" shows cans.
  3. Our voice stops too often. The hits talk in one continuous stream: about 1 to 2 pauses a minute. Our male voice E2 pauses about 27 times a minute; the public videos about 21 to 22.
  4. 3D animation is real and it works (2.1 million, 527 thousand), but only as a story: a character, a place, things happening. Blender is already installed on this laptop, so a free 3D route exists.
  5. The Hindi hit sounds like a friend telling a story: male, fast, very expressive, uses "तुम", tells a real event. Our Hindi voice is a calmer female voice reading an explanation with "आप".

What this means

  • English: E2's speed is fine. What separates it from the 11.9 M Short is the stopping: it breaks after almost every sentence. The hits run sentences into each other. Fix: shorter gaps between sentences (the voice step already has a setting for this) and scripts written as one flowing story, not separate facts.
  • Expressive voices win stories: the Jensen and 3D Wala Guru narrators rise and fall about twice as much as ours. For a story format, E2's liveliness setting can go up a step; check it stays clear.
  • Hindi: Swara flows well (no stops) but is flatter and calmer than the Hindi hit, and it is a female voice where the hit is a male storyteller. Script changes that cost nothing: use "तुम", open with a real event ("बुल्गारिया के हाईवे पर 86 गाड़ियाँ…"), and tell it as a story. Hindi words read badly in our sample (for example "fingers" came out close to "फिंगल्स") also need a pronunciation check.

How the hits open

  • Tuna: "Ever wonder exactly what's hiding inside the fish we eat all the time?" A question, but about something hidden the viewer wants to see, not the title repeated.
  • Jensen: "You are Jensen Huang, the CEO of NVIDIA…" Puts the viewer inside the story.
  • 3D Wala Guru: "In fog you follow the red light of the car ahead…" Puts the viewer in a real danger they know.
  • Ours (best): "Fresh pineapple is literally eating you back." Strong. Ours (weak): "Why do potatoes sprout shoots from those tiny dents?" The title read out again.

Matches the rule set today: a strong opening that makes people need the answer; a question only when it points at something hidden, never the title repeated.

What to change, in order

  1. Voice flow (this week): cut the gap between sentences for E2 and A1 to close to the hits' level; check every video still passes the listening check.
  2. Pictures that match the words (this week): a new clip at least every 3 to 4 seconds; each clip must show the thing being said, or the line is rewritten; no old photos or unrelated objects as fillers.
  3. Hindi script style (this week): "तुम", a real event to open, told as a story.
  4. A series hook at the end, like "next part tomorrow", for topics that have a part 2, alongside the one closing question.
  5. 3D story test (later, needs your yes): a Blender pipeline with free models and simple round-headed characters, like the 2.1 M Jensen Short. I would show it only if it truly looks as good as that one.
